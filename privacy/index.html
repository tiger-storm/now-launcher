Privacy

Privacy Policy for Now Launcher

Last updated: 12 November 2026

Now Launcher is provided by TIGERSTORM LIMITED (“we”, “us”, “our”). This policy explains what the Android app and this website process, what stays on your device, what is sent to other services, and the choices available to you.

Launcher settings and personalisation are mainly stored on your device. Online features, account services, purchases, and feedback also involve processing outside your device. We do not sell personal data. The app does not display third-party adverts.

1. Launcher Data and Device Permissions

Now Launcher uses the following information to provide its features. Optional permissions can be refused or revoked in Android settings; the related features may then be unavailable.

  • Installed apps and launcher activity: App names, package identifiers, icons, shortcuts, and available device profiles help us display and launch your apps. Records of apps opened through Now Launcher, including opening times, are stored locally to personalise suggestions. This is launcher activity, not a record of everything you do inside other apps.
  • Contacts: With permission, the app reads contact details and photos for search, favourites, and shortcuts. Favourite contact identifiers and names may be saved locally. The contact integration does not upload your address book to our servers.
  • Calendar: With permission, the app reads calendar and event details to display upcoming items. The calendar integration does not upload your calendar to our servers. Your calendar provider may separately sync your calendar.
  • Notification access and media: Access to media notifications and active media sessions supports playback information and controls. This integration processes notification and media content locally rather than sending it to our servers. Android's notification-access permission can allow access beyond the media information shown.
  • Content you create: Reminders, routines, custom cards, saved destinations, favourites, search history, settings, avatars, and customised icons are stored locally. Notifications and alarms are used to deliver timers and reminders.
  • Device controls: Features such as brightness, volume, Bluetooth, torch, and wallpaper use device settings and related permissions. Choosing an image or taking a photo for customisation may use Android's image picker or camera.

When you choose an action such as calling a contact or opening directions to an event, the relevant phone number, address, or destination is passed to the app or service that carries out the action. Its own privacy policy applies.

2. Search and Online Content

The app stores up to 50 recent searches locally to offer history suggestions. You can remove individual history entries in the app. Default Search separates the search engine from the suggestion source. Google is the default engine for submitted searches. You can choose Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo or Device default, and the app saves your selection. Suggestions default to On-device, using local history and the built-in website catalogue without sending typed text to a suggestion provider. Selecting a search engine does not enable online suggestions.

Online suggestions require a separate confirmation. If you enable them, the selected engine receives typed text and connection information such as your IP address before you submit a search. This can include text entered to find local apps or contacts. Switch back to On-device at any time to stop future requests; local results, history and submitted searches remain available. Changing engines returns suggestions to On-device. Device default supports On-device suggestions only. Search-result website icons use a local fallback in On-device mode; Online mode can request those icons from Google.

The app saves your suggestion choice on your device with the provider, confirmation wording and version, time of the change, and a revision number. Selecting On-device records that online suggestions are disabled. Online suggestions require a valid confirmation record for the selected provider.

Submitting a search or opening a link sends the query or URL to the selected browser, chosen search provider, YouTube, assistant, or other chosen service. With Device default selected, an explicitly submitted web search uses your device's search handler. The launcher does not read your browser's browsing history.

Weather requests include your chosen place name when supplied. A request without a place name allows the provider to use your IP address to estimate an area. Sports cards request fixture or race information from content services, including the team or fixture selection needed for the request. Online images and website icons are fetched from their hosts; requesting a website icon from Google sends the website address used for that icon. Website previews may also contact the website you enter.

The app does not display or request football team crests or competition emblems. Football fixture and score requests go through our backend. Other features can request images as described above.

These requests can happen when enabled cards refresh or images load, as well as when you tap an action. Their recipients can process request details and IP addresses under their own policies. Avoid entering sensitive information into online search or public feedback.

Fonts used by the app, including the Formula 1 card, are bundled with the app. Displaying text does not send a font request to Google. On-device suggestions control search suggestions; they do not disable the other online features described here.

3. Optional Sign-In and Purchases

Google Sign-In and Firebase Authentication provide optional account access. Signing in makes your Google account identifier, name, email address, and available profile details accessible to the authentication service and the app. We use account information to identify your account and link premium access; signing in does not automatically upload your launcher settings, contacts, or calendar.

Google Play processes payments, and RevenueCat manages purchase validation, subscriptions, restoration, and premium entitlements. RevenueCat processes an app user identifier, purchase and subscription records, and related device or app information. If you sign in, your Firebase user identifier is linked to RevenueCat so purchases can be associated with your account. Billing services can also operate with an SDK-generated app user identifier when you are not signed in.

Now Launcher does not receive your full payment-card number or bank credentials. Purchase and entitlement information is available to us through our billing provider and is cached by the app as needed to manage access. Using the app without an account does not prevent authentication and billing services from initialising.

Signing out clears the app's current account and cached premium-access state. If the billing service cannot complete sign-out, the app saves a local recovery flag and prevents the old billing identity from restoring access or being used for another billing action until recovery succeeds. Signing out does not delete your account, purchase records or subscription.

4. Crash Diagnostics

Now Launcher does not upload crash reports. The app does not include Google Analytics for Firebase or request Android advertising identifiers.

Google Play may separately provide crash and app-not-responding information through Android vitals when your device is eligible and you have chosen to share diagnostics with Google. This is governed by Google's policies and your device settings.

5. Feedback and Support

In-app feedback, bug reports, and feature requests send the title, description, and category you provide through our feedback service to GitLab. These requests are publicly viewable. Device model, manufacturer, brand, display, and RAM details are included only if you select “Include device details”. Do not include passwords, private contact details, health information, or other confidential material in a public report.

Our feedback service receives your IP address and derives a keyed identifier from it to limit spam. Its abuse-control records contain that identifier, request timestamps, and a fingerprint of the submitted report to detect duplicates. These records do not contain the raw IP address or report text. The identifier is pseudonymous, not necessarily anonymous. Hosting and network providers also receive connection data.

For private support or a privacy request, email hello@tigerstorm.dev. We receive your email address and the information you choose to include. Contact us if you need personal information removed from a feedback request we control; copies made by others may remain.

6. Storage, Backups, and Retention

Local app data remains until you remove it, it is replaced or pruned by the app, or you clear the app's storage or uninstall it. Removing Android permissions stops the related access but does not necessarily erase saved favourites, settings, or content you created.

Android backup and device transfer can copy app preferences and selected files, including search history, launcher activity, reminders, favourites, settings, avatars, and customised icons. Depending on your device and backup settings, copies can be held by Google or your device's backup provider and restored on reinstall or another device. Uninstalling or clearing storage does not by itself delete those backups. Use your device or backup provider's controls to manage them.

  • Accounts and purchases: Authentication records remain while your account exists, subject to deletion requests. Linked RevenueCat customer data is included in the account-deletion process described in section 9. Google Play transaction records are separate. Records needed to meet an applicable accounting, legal or dispute obligation may need to be retained for that purpose.
  • Feedback abuse controls: The service uses a rolling 24-hour window for its abuse-control records and a 10-minute window for duplicate checks. Older records are removed during subsequent processing or scheduled cleanup, including when submissions stop. These windows describe the abuse-control store, not GitLab reports or the hosting provider's operational logs.
  • Public feedback: Reports can remain available after the issue is closed or you stop using the app, so users can follow its resolution and identify duplicate issues. Contact us to request removal of personal information from reports we control. Removal cannot recall copies already made by others.
  • Private support: Retention depends on the information needed to resolve an enquiry, investigate a failure or handle a related follow-up, and any applicable legal or dispute obligations. We have not set a fixed deletion period for support emails. Provider retention settings and backups can affect when copies are removed; they do not justify keeping information we no longer need. Contact us for the retention information applicable to a particular record.

We use Android's app storage protections and HTTPS for connections to our account, billing, and feedback services. Third-party services and websites have their own security practices. No storage or transmission method can guarantee complete security.

  • Microsoft Bing — web searches and query suggestions when selected.
  • DuckDuckGo — web searches and query suggestions when selected.

Visiting it sends your IP address, requested page, and browser information to GitHub. GitHub records visitors' IP addresses for security, including when visitors are not signed in to GitHub. Its privacy statement explains its handling and retention of this information. The account-deletion page uses Firebase Authentication.

Providers may process information outside your country, including outside the UK or European Economic Area. Their processing locations, retention terms, and transfer safeguards are described in their privacy and service documentation. You can contact us for information about providers processing your data on our behalf.

8. Lawful Bases for Processing

Where the UK GDPR or EU GDPR applies, we rely on the following bases for the purposes described below, to the extent that we act as controller of that processing. A provider acting as a separate controller explains its own purposes and lawful bases in its privacy notice.

  • Providing features you request — contract, Article 6(1)(b). We process your information where necessary to provide the launcher service you request: displaying and launching apps, saving your settings and content, and carrying out searches, directions and online-content requests you choose. This basis also covers optional account authentication, purchase validation, delivery of paid features and restoration of purchased access. It does not make every optional background request necessary, or cover other people's information simply because it appears on your device.
  • Online query suggestions — consent, Article 6(1)(a). Online suggestions are disabled by default. We ask for separate confirmation before sending typed text and connection information to the selected engine. Choosing a search engine or pressing Continue during setup does not enable them. Withdraw by selecting On-device in Default Search. This stops future suggestion requests without affecting local results or your selected engine for submitted searches. Withdrawal cannot recall requests already sent and does not affect the lawfulness of processing before withdrawal. Changing engine requires a new confirmation to enable online suggestions for that provider.
  • Local personalisation — legitimate interests, Article 6(1)(f). Our interest is making the content you use on your device easier to find. Local launcher activity and recent searches support suggestions, and selected contacts and calendar integrations support access to your device's content. Safeguards include local processing, permission gates, available feature controls, removal of individual search-history entries and clearing local storage. We do not upload your address book or calendar to our servers through these integrations. Actions you choose to send to another app, online autocomplete and Android backups are separate uses described above. A device user's choice cannot supply consent on behalf of everyone whose information is on that device.
  • Entitlement checks before a purchase — legitimate interests, Article 6(1)(f). Our interest is determining which features are available and recognising existing purchases. RevenueCat can initialise and use an SDK-generated identifier before you sign in or buy anything. This identifier and related billing information are used for access management; the identifier is not necessarily anonymous.
  • Private support — legitimate interests, Article 6(1)(f). Our interest is answering enquiries and resolving problems. We use the contact details and relevant information you provide to investigate and respond. Required privacy-rights requests are handled under the legal-obligation basis below.
  • Public feedback — legitimate interests, Article 6(1)(f). Our interests are investigating bugs, planning improvements and maintaining a shared issue record so people can follow progress and avoid duplicates. The form warns that submissions are public; device details are optional and private email support is available instead. Public reports should contain only information relevant to the issue. Contact us to request removal of personal information.
  • Website delivery and service security — legitimate interests, Article 6(1)(f). Our interests are serving requested pages and content, maintaining reliable account and feedback services, and preventing abuse. Requests involve connection information and infrastructure logs. The feedback guard uses keyed IP-derived identifiers, timestamps and report fingerprints with the limited windows in section 6. Those safeguards apply to the guard, not to every provider's operational logs.
  • Privacy rights and required records — legal obligation, Article 6(1)(c). We process information where necessary to respond to applicable data-protection rights, comply with a binding legal requirement, or keep records required by applicable accounting or tax law. Identity checks are limited to what is needed to avoid disclosing information to the wrong person. This basis applies to the specific obligation, not to indefinite retention of all records.
  • Disputes and legal claims — legitimate interests, Article 6(1)(f). Our interest is establishing, exercising or defending legal claims. Processing and retention are limited to information needed for the particular dispute.

Where we rely on legitimate interests, you can object on grounds relating to your situation by emailing hello@tigerstorm.dev. We will stop the processing unless we can demonstrate overriding compelling legitimate grounds or need the information for legal claims.

Android permissions control access to device features; they are not blanket consent to all processing. Article 6 bases do not by themselves authorise processing of special-category information, such as health or religious information that might appear in contact, calendar or free-text content. Any additional conditions and controller responsibilities require separate assessment. Rules about storage or access on devices, including PECR and applicable ePrivacy rules, also apply separately.

9. Your Choices, Account Deletion, and Rights

  • Review or revoke optional permissions in Android settings, and disable optional cards in the app where available.
  • Use the launcher without Google sign-in, remove individual search-history entries, or clear local app data through Android settings.
  • Choose On-device under Suggestions in Default Search to stop online query suggestions. You can still use local results and explicitly submit searches.
  • Manage Android backups using the controls available on your device or Google account.
  • Delete your Firebase sign-in account and linked RevenueCat customer data using the app's “Delete Account” link or the account-deletion page. Use the same Google account you used in Now Launcher.

Account deletion, clearing local storage, removing a backup, and deleting public feedback are separate actions. Account deletion removes linked RevenueCat customer data before deleting Firebase authentication. If either step fails, the page will ask you to retry rather than confirm completion. Close the app before deletion; signing in or restoring purchases later may create new billing records. This action does not erase local launcher data, backups, public reports or Google Play transaction records. Email us to request deletion of other personal data we control and to discuss any records that must be retained. We may need to verify your identity.

Deleting your account or uninstalling the app does not cancel a subscription. Manage or cancel it through Google Play subscriptions.

Depending on the law that applies to you, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict processing of, or receive a portable copy of your personal data. You may withdraw consent where processing relies on consent. Contact us to exercise these rights or raise a concern.

You may also object to processing based on legitimate interests. You can complain to your local data protection authority, including the UK's Information Commissioner's Office.
